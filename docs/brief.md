# Prompt for the next attempt — Text Voxel v2 (renderer rebuild)

Paste everything below the line into a fresh Claude Code session in `~/claudecode`.

---

We are rebuilding the renderer of an existing single-file browser game from scratch while keeping its entire world simulation intact, and the target this time is a deliberate, art-directed ASCII look that stands next to the terminal RPG Swaggerfall and beats it, rather than the soft pixel-mosaic the current version ended up with, which reads more like a child's Paint drawing than a designed retro world. Before you write any code at all, research the problem online and come back with a written visual direction and static mockups for me to choose from; I will say which direction to build, and only then should you touch the engine.

The current state of the project is a finished, working single HTML file at `~/claudecode/text-voxel/index.html`, about 2,300 lines and 115 KB, with no dependencies and no build step, committed to the public repo github.com/OddSlice/text-voxel and live on GitHub Pages at https://oddslice.github.io/text-voxel/ (append `?seed=42` for the world in its screenshots). Read that file and `~/claudecode/text-voxel/docs/project.md` in full before planning anything, because the world simulation in it is good and is not what we are replacing. That simulation is a seeded 512×512 wrapping heightmap built from home-made value noise with ridged mountains, a sea at the 25th percentile of heights, two rivers carved by a steepest-descent walk, materials for water, grass, two grass tones, rock, snow, road, stone, wood and leaf, castles in three designs (a small one, a 21×21 keep castle and a 27×27 three-ring concentric castle) plus towers with spiral stairs, ruins and standing-stone circles, each built as a small voxel template anchored to levelled ground with real walkable interiors, an A* road network joining every landmark with fords where it must cross water, roughly 5,600 trees, bushes and boulders placed by a forest-density noise and bucketed by 32 cells, bird flocks, drifting clouds, four merchants who walk the roads and can be talked to through an HTML trade panel with gold, torches at gates and campfires at the stone circles, grounded first-person movement with gravity, jump, sprint, swept wall and slope collision and correct floor resolution inside towers, an `F` fly/noclip toggle, an adjustable view distance and a monospace HUD. All of that must survive the rebuild essentially untouched; the debug handle `window.TV` exposes `world`, `cam`, `player`, `keys`, `ui`, `view`, `light`, `stats`, `update(dt)`, `groundAt`, `setMode`, `setHour` and `regenerate(seed)`, and the scripted-walk tests that verified movement lean on `TV.update(dt)` plus `TV.keys`, so keep that surface.

What is being thrown away is the renderer, which currently raymarches the heightmap and structure voxels per screen column into a grid of roughly 4×7 pixel cells where each cell carries a single glyph and a single colour, composes that into an ImageData buffer in JavaScript and blits it once per frame, with continuous shading from a 16-step material ramp, 8-step distance fog, a blended day/dusk/night palette, screen-space water reflections and point lights. It performs fine — about 4 ms of raymarch plus 2 ms of compose at 1440×900 in Chromium, 60 fps in Firefox and WebKit too — but it looks like blurred mush because every cell is effectively one flat colour, the glyph weave carries no shape information, and nothing about the palette or the glyph choice was art-directed.

Your research phase should start with Swaggerfall by holonaut64 on itch.io, a first-person open-world fantasy RPG that renders in the terminal, written in Rust with the Ratatui library and exported to the web through WebAssembly, whose developer explicitly notes the graphics are ASCII and limited to what the command line offers, "luckily this includes background colors for text glyphs", and whose devlogs mention supersampling, shadows, godrays, reworked twilight sky colouring and cloud lighting, and frame-time work to pay for those effects. Watch the trailer and the longer gameplay video, read the itch page and every devlog, and study frames from the TikTok and YouTube shorts that made it trend, and write down specifically what makes it read as a crafted image: how dense the cell grid is relative to the screen, how many colours are on screen at once, how edges and silhouettes are drawn, how it handles sky gradients, fog, night, interiors and faces. Then research the underlying craft rather than just that one game: the Unicode block elements range U+2580–U+259F and the standard trick of pairing a half block with distinct foreground and background colours so that one cell carries two independently coloured pixels and doubles vertical resolution, the quadrant characters that give 2×2 per cell, the sextant and Braille tricks that give 2×3 and 2×4, ordered and blue-noise dithering between two palette colours to get apparent shades a fixed palette does not contain, edge-detection approaches where the glyph is chosen from the local image gradient so that `─ │ / \ ⟋` trace silhouettes instead of density ramps smearing them, classic ANSI and PETSCII art and demoscene text-mode work for palette and composition discipline, and how curated 16-colour palettes are built. Look at what image-to-ASCII tools and terminal image viewers do well and badly. You have web search and fetch; use them properly and cite what you found.

The deliverable of that phase, before any engine work, is a short written direction document plus two or three visual mockups I can compare. Build the mockups cheaply and honestly: load the existing `index.html` world in a headless browser via playwright-cli, dump a frame's worth of world data (per-column terrain distance, height, material, shade, and the sprite and structure hits) from the same fixed camera and seed, then render that same data offline into candidate PNGs at 1440×900 under each candidate technique, so I am comparing real scenes from our world and not concept art. Each candidate should state its cell grid, its palette, how many colours per cell, which glyph set it uses and how it picks glyphs, and its honest cost estimate. Make one of them genuinely ambitious rather than three safe variations.

Once I pick a direction, build it as a new single self-contained file at `~/claudecode/text-voxel/v2.html`, leaving `index.html` and the live Pages build alone as the baseline, so the two can be opened side by side and the old one keeps working; port the world simulation across wholesale rather than rewriting it, and keep the same controls, HUD and trade panel. The expected end state is a browser world that still runs from one file with no dependencies, generates in about a second, holds 60 fps at 1440×900 in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, and looks unmistakably like a hand-crafted text-mode world rather than a smeared mosaic: crisp silhouettes on trees, castles and merchants, a palette someone clearly chose, skies and fog that read as deliberate, and night lighting where the torches and campfires carry real atmosphere.

Watch out for several traps this project has already hit. The block glyphs of the chosen font rasterise solid at small sizes, so `▓` at four pixels is indistinguishable from `█`, which means subcell block glyphs must be hand-built as masks rather than trusted to the font; the colour packer must clamp every channel, because an unclamped brightness multiply once wrapped the sky to yellow; drawing off-pixels in pure black makes the weave far too loud, so any dimming must be a scaled copy of the cell colour or a real second colour; WebKit snaps text baselines to whole pixels, which is why the current renderer composes pixels itself instead of issuing text calls, and any return to `fillText` will hit that and will also be far too slow at small cells; Pointer Lock is refused inside the Claude desktop browser pane, so real mouse-look must be verified with playwright-cli in Chromium or Firefox, where it works; and every playwright-cli call takes a second or two, so anything time-dependent, such as merchants walking at three cells a second, has to be frozen between calls or the thing you meant to photograph has wandered off. The `fps` launch config already serves `~/claudecode` on port 8090, so the page is at `http://localhost:8090/text-voxel/v2.html`, and the scripted movement suites from the previous build are worth re-running against the new file to prove the simulation survived the port.

Work phase by phase the way we always do: a short design pass that states the open numbers and your recommendation, one confirmation from me, then build and verify with real screenshots before moving on.
