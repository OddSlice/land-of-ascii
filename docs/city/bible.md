# Port Ascii: art bible

The second world of Ascii, **Port Ascii**: a cyberpunk city in the spirit of Night City, with elevation and megabuildings, chosen at the start alongside the Land (Martin, 30 Sep 2026). On 4 Oct Martin asked for it to be planned properly from the ground up, starting with a design system, so that it looks consistent and its parts are easy to tell apart. This file is that design system: what the city looks like, why, and the rules every part of it follows.

- **Status:** step 0 is done, and Martin settled its open questions on 4 Oct (see [Decisions](#16-decisions)). Nothing in the game uses it yet.
- **The research behind it:** [research.md](research.md).
- **The colours:** [palette.json](palette.json), generated by `node tools/city-palette.mjs` from a recipe, so the whole palette moves together when one number changes.
- **The pictures:** `node tools/city-sheet.mjs` draws them into `shots/city/step0/`:
  - [sheet-city](../../shots/city/step0/sheet-city.png): the city on its hill and the four districts;
  - [sheet-moods](../../shots/city/step0/sheet-moods.png): the three moods step 1 will test;
  - [sheet-palette](../../shots/city/step0/sheet-palette.png): every ramp, and the colour checks.

  They are diagrams drawn from the palette, not renders. In phase 1 we chose the Land's look from real frames of the engine, not from concept art, and step 1 does the same for the city.

**For Claude, before touching anything in the city:** read this whole file. Every building, sign, light, person and screen follows it. If something you want to build breaks a rule here, ask Martin and change this file first; never bend a rule quietly.

## 1. The city in a paragraph

Port Ascii is a harbour city climbing a steep hill. There is no room to spread, so it builds upward, and money climbs with it: the corporations own the summit and the poor live by the water. It is always wet, often raining, and never quite dark, because the signs light it all night. You arrive at the docks and look up at the whole city rising in front of you.

## 2. Three words to test everything against

1. **Towering.** Taller than it is wide. The sky is a strip between buildings, and you look up a lot. *Does this make the city feel taller?*
2. **Rain-soaked.** Wet surfaces, lights mirrored in them, rain, mist. The city looks its best wet, at night. *Does this still look right wet, at night, in the rain?*
3. **Readable.** However dense it gets, you always know where you can go, what you can use and which district you are in. *Can someone tell what this is, and where they are, in one second?*

## 3. How it differs from the Land

| | The Land | The city |
|---|---|---|
| Light | sun by day, warm fire at night | smog by day, neon at night |
| Colours | greens, ochres, warm stone | dark, cool neutrals, plus one neon colour per district |
| Shapes | round hills and trees, pitched roofs | straight lines, verticals, repetition |
| Sky | wide and open, clouds, stars | a strip between towers, a low cloud lit from below, no stars |
| Ground | grass, rock, dirt roads | asphalt, concrete, stairs, wet everywhere |
| People | villagers, farmers, guards | people under umbrellas, vendors, corporate guards, dock workers |
| Screens | warm brown panels, amber highlight | dark glass panels, warm-white highlight |

**The same in both:** the text-cell renderer and its marks, both looks (painted and mosaic), the controls, your hero, the layout of the screens, your bag.

## 4. What you must be able to read, in order

Valve called this the "read hierarchy" when they made Team Fortress 2: decide what the player needs to see first, and give each thing its own signal. Anything not on this list belongs to the quiet base.

1. **Where can I walk?** Brightness. Walkable ground is lit (street lights, the spill of signs, wet reflections); drops and walls are darker. Walkways and stairs are lit along their edges by the district's own street light.
2. **What can I use?** The signal: the one warm white, on doorways you can go through, vendors' work lamps and lifts. It is brighter than any neon and breathes slowly when you are near, and the E prompt shows, as in the Land.
3. **Who is that?** Silhouette first (coat, umbrella, helmet, hard hat), one accent colour second.
4. **Where am I?** The district's colour, its building shapes, its landmark on the skyline, and the height: the higher you are, the colder and cleaner the light.
5. **When is it?** The sky, the haze, the rain.

## 5. Colour

The engine already works like a design system: hand-built colour ramps for each hour (day, dusk, night), and every surface drawn from its material's ramp. The city gets its own set in the same form (dark to light, the shadows leaning cool), so it blends through the day the way the Land's does.

### Every colour has a job

| Job | Colours | Share of the screen |
|---|---|---|
| The quiet base | concrete, stained concrete, steel, glass, asphalt, pale tile, brick, rust, plastic, water | 80–90% |
| District light | one neon per district: orange, green, magenta, cyan | 10–20% |
| You can use this | the signal: one warm white | 1–2% |
| Windows | warm (homes and shops), cool (offices) | part of the base |
| Air | sky, haze, low cloud, rain | |

- **Materials change with the hour; lights don't.** Neon, the signal and lit windows keep their colours day and night. The hour changes only how strongly they shine: 0.3 by day, 0.8 at dusk, fully at night.
- **Each neon has five steps:** two spill tints (what it does to the wall and the street around it), the glow, the bright tube and the near-white core.
- **The signal can't be a hue,** because every hue on the wheel already belongs to a district. So "you can use this" is:
  - brightness: the brightest thing at street level, at least 0.08 lighter than any neon glow;
  - a slow breathing (two seconds, never a blink) when you are near;
  - a shape: a lit doorway, a lamp.

  The screens use the same warm white for anything you can click, so it means one thing everywhere.
- **The neon colours are spread round the colour wheel and stepped in lightness** (green brightest, magenta darkest), so they stay apart in greyscale and for colour-blind eyes (see [the checks](#14-the-checks)).
- **On-screen budget:** no more colours at once than the Land shows, about 80. In phase 1, v1 showed about 4,000 colours and read as mush.

### The moods to test (step 1)

The same materials, districts and neon under three different lights ([sheet-moods](../../shots/city/step0/sheet-moods.png)). Martin picked A; step 1 still shows B and C beside it in the engine, since a palette swap costs nothing and the engine may surprise us:

- **A, Rain noir** (Martin's pick): blue-black nights, grey smog days, the neon held back; rain most nights.
- **B, Neon soak:** violet-black nights, stronger and wetter neon, more signs to a street. ⚠️ The risk is mush.
- **C, Smog amber:** amber haze by day and at dusk, warmer nights, the neon cooler against it; drier.

## 6. The districts

**Wealth = height.** The four districts step up the hill ([sheet-city](../../shots/city/step0/sheet-city.png)). Each has its own neon, materials, shapes, signs, street furniture and landmark, so you can tell them apart by colour, by shape, or by the skyline alone.

### The Docks (the bottom: industry and the harbour)

- **Light:** sodium orange. Tall street lamps throw orange pools; work lights.
- **Materials:** rust, corrugated metal, stained concrete, asphalt, black water.
- **Shapes:** long low sheds with saw-tooth roofs, stacked containers, chimneys with steam, piers. Wide and low.
- **Signs:** stencilled codes on the walls (GATE 3, P-12), few and plain.
- **Street:** bollards, chains, crates, puddles, steam vents.
- **People:** dock workers in hard hats and orange vests, sailors.
- **Vendors:** tools, fish, salvage.
- **Landmark:** a giant gantry crane at the water's edge, its boom over the harbour and its lamps mirrored in the water.
- **Feels like:** a port at 3 a.m. *Heavy · rusty · wide.*

### The Stacks (the lower slopes: homes)

- **Light:** fluorescent tube green, in stairwells and corridors lit by old tubes.
- **Materials:** stained concrete, old brick, rusted balconies.
- **Shapes:** huge blocks, 15 to 30 storeys, flat tops with water tanks and aerials; repeating rows of windows; walkways between the blocks at several heights; washing lines.
- **Signs:** big painted block numbers and a few small hand-made signs.
- **Street:** lanes (never narrower than 7 cells: the camera needs it), bins, pipes, air-conditioning units, puddles.
- **Windows:** thousands, warm and dim; about a third lit at night.
- **People:** the people who live there, doing their washing and going to the shops.
- **Vendors:** groceries, repairs, second-hand goods.
- **Landmark:** the biggest block, its number lit huge (BLOCK 9).
- **Feels like:** a thousand lit windows. *Dense · repeated · lived-in.*

### Neon Row (the middle: shops, food, nights out)

- **Light:** magenta, the busiest neon in the city.
- **Materials:** plastic panels, tiles, glass shopfronts, concrete.
- **Shapes:** mid-rise blocks (4 to 10 storeys), uneven, wrapped in signs: vertical blades sticking out over the street, awnings, outside stairs.
- **Signs:** more than anywhere else: stacked vertical signs, at least one to a shop, in real words.
- **Street:** noodle stalls with steam rising, vending machines, crowds.
- **People:** the most people at night, and vendors under their work lamps.
- **Vendors:** food (noodles, dumplings), drinks, clothes.
- **Landmark:** a giant hologram jellyfish drifting slowly over the main crossing.
- **Feels like:** a night market in the rain. *Loud · shiny · crowded.*

### The Spires (the summit: corporations)

- **Light:** ice cyan and cold white.
- **Materials:** dark glass, polished steel, pale stone plazas.
- **Shapes:** tall, slender towers with setbacks and sharp tops; sky bridges; wide, empty plazas.
- **Signs:** few and huge: one word high on a tower, thin lines of light along the edges. No clutter.
- **Street:** planters, benches, bollards, security gates.
- **Windows:** office windows in cool grids; white strip lights.
- **People:** few. Corporate guards (helmets, long coats, a cyan accent), office workers under black umbrellas.
- **Vendors:** expensive gear, coffee.
- **Landmark:** the tallest tower, rising into the cloud and ringed with light just below it. Visible from everywhere in the city, it does what the castles do in the Land.
- **Feels like:** a bank lobby at midnight. *Cold · quiet · expensive.*

### Across the city

- **The elevated rail** climbs the hill from the Docks to the Spires through every district: a line to steer by, and a way to see the city from above. (A later step.)
- **Stair-streets:** where a street gets too steep it becomes stairs, kept under the Land's walking limit (a rise of 1.5 per cell).
- **The harbour:** black water mirroring the city, with the same soft reflections as the Land's water.
- **Where two districts meet,** their colours may mix for a block or two. Nowhere else.

## 7. Shapes

- **Straight lines and right angles everywhere,** the opposite of the Land's curves. Curves only on Neon Row's signs, the hologram and people.
- **Repetition is the city's texture:** windows in grids, balconies, containers, sheds.
- **Every district's skyline reads on its own,** in black against the sky:
  - the Docks: low and wide, with the crane;
  - the Stacks: big flat-topped blocks with aerials;
  - Neon Row: jagged with sign blades;
  - the Spires: thin needles.
- **The skyline steps up the hill:** the higher the ground, the taller the buildings.
- **Landmarks have shapes nothing else has.**
- **References give us shapes, never colours.** Ghost in the Shell's location photos of Hong Kong were taken in black and white, so they would "only show the build architecture", and the art director chose the colours separately. Here, all colour comes from the palette.

## 8. Light and weather

- **Neon is information.** Only signs, the trims of important buildings, landmarks, and a district's stairwell and street lights glow. Never plain walls, and never a glowing outline on every edge.
- **Light spills:** a sign tints the wall and the street around it in its colour (the spill steps), fading within a few cells.
- **Wet streets mirror the signs softly,** by the same angle rule as the Land's water (`fresnel`): strongest looking along the street, faint looking down at your feet.
- **Windows:** warm and dim in homes and shops, cool grids in offices; at night about a third are lit.
- **Street lights by district:**
  - the Docks: sodium orange;
  - the Stacks: green-white tubes;
  - Neon Row: none needed, the signs light it;
  - the Spires: white strips.
- **The hours:** by day, grey smog with the signs dimmed; at dusk the smog turns orange and violet as the signs come on; the night belongs to the neon. It runs on the same 24-hour clock as the Land, so people's timetables work the same way.
- **Slow motion only:** signs never blink. A few big signs scroll their text slowly, the hologram drifts, rain falls, steam rises. Nothing flickers.
- **Rain:** most nights, and the streets stay damp all night after it stops. Mist settles low over the Docks at dawn. No snow.

## 9. Surfaces and marks (the two looks)

Every new material needs, in both looks:

- its ramps for day, dusk and night ([palette.json](palette.json));
- its marks in the mosaic look: a small vocabulary of its own, fixed to the world and fading with distance, as the Land's grass tufts and mortar lines do;
- its pattern in the painted look, where it has one (window grids, panel seams).

The first vocabulary (drawn as glyph bitmasks in code, never a font):

| Material | Marks |
|---|---|
| concrete | stains, hairline cracks, drips under ledges |
| glass | window grids; lit panes filled |
| steel | panel seams, rivets |
| asphalt | lane dashes, drains, puddle strokes that carry reflections |
| brick | courses (like the Land's mortar lines) |
| rust | vertical ribs (corrugated metal), streaks |
| tile | a square grid |
| signs | letters (below) |
| rain | streaks (the Land's), splashes in puddles |
| cables | thin diagonals across the streets (Neon Row, the Stacks) |

### Signs are text

The city is drawn in letters, so its signs say real words, **in English and in Cyrillic** (Bulgarian). Each sign is in one script, and both scripts appear in every district. Up close you can read them; far off they become blocks of glow, as the Land's marks fade.

A first vocabulary (Martin to check the Bulgarian):

| District | English | Cyrillic |
|---|---|---|
| the Docks | GATE 3, DOCK 7, P-12 | СКЛАД 3 (warehouse), ДОК 7, ВХОД (entrance) |
| the Stacks | BLOCK 9, REPAIR | БЛОК 9, МАГАЗИН (shop), АПТЕКА (pharmacy, with its green cross), РЕМОНТ (repair) |
| Neon Row | NOODLES, BAR, HOTEL, OPEN, 24H | ДЮНЕР (döner), БАР, ХОТЕЛ, КАФЕ, ДЕНОНОЩНО (open all night) |
| the Spires | TOWER ONE, a corporation's name | КУЛА (tower) |

The letters are bitmaps drawn in code, like every glyph: A to Z, 0 to 9, and the 30 capitals of the Bulgarian alphabet (about 19 of them have shapes Latin lacks: Б Г Д Ж З И Й Л П У Ф Ц Ч Ш Щ Ъ Ь Ю Я).

Each district has its own sign style:

- the Spires: one word in thin, spaced capitals;
- Neon Row: stacked vertical blades;
- the Stacks: big painted numbers;
- the Docks: stencilled codes.

⚠️ Whether the engine can draw readable letters on a sign in both looks is one of step 1's tests.

## 10. People and vehicles

- **Role by silhouette first, one accent second:**
  - people in the street: long coats, hoods, **umbrellas** (the city's silhouette in the rain; mostly dark, a few clear ones that take the colour of the nearest sign);
  - vendors: aprons, under their warm-white work lamp (the signal);
  - corporate guards: helmets with visors, long coats, a cyan accent; in the Spires and where districts meet;
  - dock workers: hard hats and orange vests (the Docks' colour);
  - the crowd on Neon Row at night: brighter clothes, magenta accents.
- **Coats come from the base** (charcoal, olive, wine, navy), so crowds stay quiet and the accents read.
- **Vehicles:** flying cars as slow lights in lanes high above (as the Land has birds), and the elevated rail. No cars in the streets: the streets are for walking.

## 11. The screens

- **The same layout and behaviour as the Land's:** the legend bottom left, your bag on the right, wares on the left, the HUD. Only the skin changes. The colours of the screens are CSS variables, and each world sets its own.
- **The city's skin:**
  - dark glass panels (near-black blue, a little see-through), cool light-grey text, thin steel borders;
  - **the signal's warm white for anything you can click or hover**, the job amber does in the Land;
  - the current district's name on the HUD, in its colour.
- **Icons:** the same 16×16 pixel style, drawn in the city's palette.
- **Money:** credits instead of gold.

## 12. Never

1. **No rainbow mush:** at most two neon colours in one view (a district's own and, where it meets another, its neighbour's).
2. **No neon on plain walls,** and no glowing outline on every edge.
3. **No blinking or flashing.** Nothing moves fast but rain and traffic.
4. **No synthwave:** no grid floors, no sunset gradients, no palm trees.
5. **No pure black, no pure white** (the Land's rule too).
6. **Colour is never the only clue:** every district also differs in its shapes and signs.
7. **No real brands, logos or names** from Cyberpunk 2077, Blade Runner or anywhere else. The repository is public. Inspired by, never copied.
8. **No random letter soup on signs:** they say real words.
9. **No more colours on screen than the Land** (about 80).
10. **No walkable street narrower than 7 cells and no gate narrower than 5,** the Land's rule for the camera.

## 13. The checklist for anything new

Every future step goes through this list:

- Which district is it in? Does it use only that district's neon plus the base?
- In black against the sky, would you know what it is?
- Brightness: the base stays dark; neon only on a sign, a trim or a landmark; the warm white only on something you can use.
- Does it look right wet, at night, and on a smoggy day?
- Does it have its ramps, its mosaic marks and its painted pattern?
- Have the lookbook shots been taken again? Do the four checks pass? Are the colours on screen within the budget, and does it hold 60 fps?

## 14. The checks

These run on every sheet from step 1 on (a tool for the first three comes with the lookbook):

1. **Greyscale:** in black and white, the districts still look different, by shape and brightness.
2. **Blur:** blurred by about 1% of the picture's width, the important things still stand out: the walkable path, the signal, the landmarks.
3. **Where am I:** from street shots taken at random spots and shown without labels, you can name the district.
4. **Colour blindness:** protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, simulated after Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009).
   - The four district glows must stay at least 0.12 apart (in OKLab, where 0 is the same colour) for normal, protanopic and deuteranopic eyes. About 1 man in 12 has red-green colour blindness (protan or deutan).
   - Tritanopia (rare) and greyscale are reported; shapes carry them.
   - The signal must be lighter than every neon glow by at least 0.08.

Today, for direction A:

| Eyes | Closest pair | Apart | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| normal | green / cyan | 0.255 | pass |
| protanopia | green / cyan | 0.249 | pass |
| deuteranopia | orange / green | 0.151 | pass |
| tritanopia | green / cyan | 0.076 | reported |
| greyscale | orange / magenta | 0.079 | reported |

The signal leads the brightest glow by 0.083 (pass). `node tools/city-sheet.mjs` prints these and fails if a rule breaks.
